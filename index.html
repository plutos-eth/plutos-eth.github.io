01

THE TOOL

Two halves. One rule engine.

banana is a desktop terminal, not a website and not a bot you rent. It runs on your machine and talks to nothing but the RPC endpoint you configure.

01 / SNIPER

Watches every launch, live.

It sees each pons v2 launch as it happens, evaluates it against a transparent boolean filter, waits until the opening tax decays under a ceiling you set, and buys. Every refusal is recorded with the rule that caused it.

  • Transparent boolean filter — no score, no black box
  • Waits out tax decay before it prices the order
  • TEST mode rehearses the whole pipeline against the real chain

02 / STRATEGY LAB

Indexes history, offline.

The Lab indexes launch history to local SQLite, runs the same filter against it with no network at all, and shows what actually happened to the tokens that passed — including the ones that went to zero.

  • Funnel view that shows the denominator, not just the winners
  • Rule editor sits beside the backtest it feeds
  • Honesty guards printed on the screen, every run

THE LINK BETWEEN THEM

One strategy.json. Two consumers. No translation step.

The same file you tune in the Lab is the file the sniper trades from. Nothing is re-typed, re-interpreted or re-approximated on the way across — which is the only reason a backtest tells you anything about the thing that will run tonight. 

crates/core      the rule engine and curve maths
crates/chain     JSON-RPC and the priority gate
crates/store     SQLite — history.db, live.db
crates/indexer   four-phase history indexer
crates/backtest  the Lab: funnel, outcomes, guards
crates/live      the only crate that may hold a key

HONESTY GUARDS

The screen argues with you on purpose.

Every number a backtest produces is easy to read as a promise. These three live in the primary reading path so that it cannot happen quietly.

  1. 01
    The headline is never a peak.

    max_multiple is labelled “max achievable / peak” and never “profit”. The headline is the fixed-hold multiple — what a rule you could actually have executed would have produced.

  2. 02
    A backtest is evidence about one window.

    If the whole chain was rising across the window you indexed, every strategy looks good. The Lab says so on the screen, every time.

  3. 03
    It does not detect rugs, honeypots or scams.

    It reads what a launch declared about itself and what its deployer did before. That is all it reads, and it says so.

FAQ

Questions the banana gets a lot.

Short answers about what the coin is, what the software reads, and what both of them deliberately refuse to do.

Do I need $BANANA to use the terminal?

No. The terminal is MIT licensed and free, and it has no token gate, no licence check and no account. It does not know whether you hold anything. The coin is a joke about the chain it runs on, and the software would work identically if the coin had never existed.

Does banana detect rugs, honeypots or scams?

No, and it never claims to. It reads what a launch declared about itself and what its deployer did before. That is the whole input. A launch can pass every rule you wrote and still go to zero — the Lab shows you exactly those launches on purpose.

Does it connect to my wallet?

There is no wallet connection, no signer prompt and no browser extension. LIVE mode reads a key from a file on your own disk, in plaintext, and signs locally. Nothing leaves your machine except JSON-RPC to the endpoint you configured yourself.

What does “nothing leaves your machine” actually mean?

No telemetry, no vendor server, no third-party price API and no account. A launch’s calldata contains an attacker-controlled logo URL; if the webview could fetch it, a deployer would learn the IP of every banana user watching their launch, in real time, before they buy. The Content-Security-Policy forbids outbound requests and CI compares every directive exactly on each build.

Where does my data live?

%APPDATA%\banana on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/banana on macOS, and $XDG_DATA_HOME/banana (or ~/.local/share/banana) on Linux. It holds the indexed chain facts, your positions and every refusal, your strategy.json, and — only if you set one — your key.

What will it refuse to do?

Two gaps are code that is not written yet, and you will meet them. A position that graduates to a Uniswap v4 pool while you hold it: the program refuses to sell and tells you to sell by hand. And launches paired against anything other than ETH — roughly 60% of them — are refused by name, because a non-ETH quote needs allowance handling the program does not do.

Is this affiliated with pons, Uniswap or Robinhood?

No. banana is an independent client that reads public chain data and submits ordinary transactions. The name, the artwork and the Robin Hood theme are a joke about the chain this runs on — not a claim of any relationship with anyone, and nothing here is licensed from or approved by the projects named above.

How do I know I have the right contract address?

Take it from this page or from the project’s X account, and from nowhere else. Both will publish it at the same moment. Any address circulated before that — in a DM, a reply, a group chat — is not the token, whatever it is named. Compare every character, not the first four.
